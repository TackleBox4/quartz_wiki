
## Core combat format

Classic turn-based combat.

Every combatant has Speed.

Speed determines individual turn order.

The complete current timeline is visible to the player.

Turn order can be manipulated by skills and swapping.

After everyone acts, the next round’s order is recalculated from Speed.

The player can inspect other characters’ combat information during another character’s turn.

Four party members participate and can act, including reserve characters.

## Party formation

 Party is divided into two fixed pairs:

   Left-front and left-reserve.
   
   Right-front and right-reserve.

 Pair arrangements are chosen outside combat.

 Characters cannot move between the left and right pairs during normal combat.

 They can only exchange front/reserve positions with their existing partner.

 The party returns to its predetermined layout after combat, regardless of how the encounter ended.

 Front and reserve characters both receive turns.

 Position changes skill availability and effects.

 Characters equip one shared loadout rather than separate front/reserve loadouts.

 Some skills are front-only.

 Some skills are reserve-only.

 Some are available in both positions but behave differently.

## Normal swapping

 One manual swap can be used per turn.
 
 Manual swapping does not consume the character’s action.
 
 The two paired characters exchange front/reserve positions.
 
 Their positions on the current turn timeline are also exchanged.
 
 Next round’s initiative is still recalculated normally through Speed.
 
 Some skills include a swap as part of their effect.
 
 Skill-triggered swaps can occur beyond the normal manual swap allowance.

## Forced movement

 Certain enemies can push or pull characters between front and reserve.
 
 Forced movement does not change positions on the turn timeline.
 
 Forced movement does not activate swap attacks or voluntary swap effects.
 
 Reserve characters remain able to act even after being forcibly moved.

## Enemy formation

 Ordinary enemies cannot use the party’s advanced front/reserve tactics.
 
 This is treated as an explicit combat rule.
 
 Exceptional enemies may violate this rule.
 
 Exact maximum enemy count is not locked.
 
 Four to six simultaneous enemies is the current provisional range.

## Universal turn commands

 Attack.

 Skill menu.

 Swap.

 Item.

 Interaction menu:

   Escape.
   Talk.

 Synergy Swap.

 Defensive play comes from skills, positioning, parries, shields, concealment and other character mechanics.

## Synergy Swaps

 Comparable to Persona 5 Showtime attacks.

 Six planned Synergy Swaps: one for every possible pairing of the four characters.

 Each fixed left/right pair has a separate synergy meter.

 The meter usually fills when either member:

   Uses skills.
   
   Inflicts damage.

 Activation requirements may vary between pairings.

 When activated:

   The pair performs a unique cinematic cooperative attack.
   
   It deals heavy damage.
   
   The two characters exchange front/reserve positions.

 Only the two pairings selected before combat are available during that encounter.

 Changing pair combinations normally requires leaving combat.

## Death, defeat and emergency formation

 Characters cannot be revived during combat.

 Organic characters must be resurrected at the Hub.

 The Robot must be repaired at the Hub.

 If a front character dies:

   Their reserve partner is forcibly moved into the front.
   
   This does not trigger voluntary swap effects.

 Combat continues while at least one party member remains standing.

 All four party members being defeated causes game over.

 Non-scripted encounters can be escaped at any point.

 Scripted encounters cannot be escaped normally.

 If only two characters remain:

   The surviving left-side character moves behind the right-side survivor.
   
   They become a new front/reserve pair.
   
   Their new Synergy Swap meter begins at zero.

## Talk interactions

 Talk consumes the acting character’s full turn.

 Outcomes depend primarily on the enemy rather than the speaking character.

 Possible outcomes include:

   Learning information.
   
   Making the encounter easier.
   
   Receiving items.
   
   Ending combat completely.

 Different enemy types respond differently:

   Humans can potentially be reasoned with.
   
   Automatons respond according to their directives and intelligence.
   
   Animals and monsters cannot be approached in the same way.

 Conditions vary by enemy:

   Some are easier to talk to before being attacked.
   
   Attacking may remove peaceful options.
   
   Some become more willing to negotiate at low health.

## Health and recovery

 No automatic health recovery after combat.

 Health carries between encounters.

 Healing can come from:

   Items.
   
   Healing spells.
   
   Character-specific self-healing skills.
   
   Certain mods or sigils.

 Items and some spells can be used both during and between encounters.

 Each character generally has a self-healing action.

 Self-healing usually requires an enemy target and cannot simply be used freely outside combat.

 Mods are Robot-exclusive.

 Sigils are used by the organic characters.

 Certain mods or sigils can provide post-combat recovery.

## Experience and skill progression

 Character levels provide skill-tree progression.

 Additional skills can be:

   Learned from items found in the world.
   
   Taught by people inside the Academy.

 Everyone present in an encounter gains equal experience.

 Experience is not divided according to damage or participation.

 A character defeated during the encounter still receives experience.

 A character already dead and absent before combat receives none.

 Characters prepare a limited skill loadout.

 Each character has one shared loadout across both positions.

 Position determines availability or behavior of equipped skills.

 Core character mechanics do not consume skill slots.

## Elements and combat conditions

 Enemies normally have fixed elemental weaknesses and resistances.

 Temporary effects can create additional interactions.

 Example:

   Apply oil.
   
   Follow with fire damage.

 Elemental access includes:

   Old Man’s elemental spells.
   
   Girl’s weapon effects.
   
   Tinkerer’s elemental contraptions.
   
   Robot’s on-hit effects from enchantments, consumables or mods.

 Weakness information may be discovered through:

   Environmental clues.
   
   Talk.
   
   Analysis skills.
   
   Experimenting with attacks.

 The Tinkerer maintains an enemy logbook containing discovered combat information.

## Breakable weak points

 Weak points are predefined parts of certain enemies.

They begin hidden.

Some enemies have no weak points.

Analysis-type skills reveal them.

Revealed weak points can be targeted by specific attacks.

Most have one hit point.

Stronger weak points may have two or three hit points.

Breaking one produces an enemy-specific benefit.

Examples already discussed:

Breaking a leg.

Snapping off an armor brace.

Characters other than the Robot can interact with weak points.

The Girl’s sniper counterpart can reveal or target them.

The Tinkerer’s high-precision turret can target them.
