
## Base core mechanic: sleep meter

 Sleep meter rises slightly after every turn.

 Skills add tiredness according to their individual cost.

 Tiredness functions similarly to mana expenditure.

 Certain skills force him to sleep immediately.

 At maximum sleep:

   Old Man loses consciousness.
   
   Alter takes control for one turn.
   
   Alter autonomously selects both spell and target.
   
   Alter usually attacks enemies with powerful spells.
   
   Player cannot control this turn.

 Old Man returns after the alter’s single turn.

## Position roles

### Front

 Destructive spellcaster.
 
 Direct magical damage dealer.

### Reserve

 Weapon enchanting.
 
 Creates on-hit effects for other characters.
 
 Enchantment duration depends on the skill.
 
 Most last several turns.
 
 On-hit effects can activate through:

   Normal attacks.
 
   Robot deflect counterattacks.
   
   Other qualifying multi-hit abilities.

## Teleport swap

 Works in both directions.
 
 Swaps the Old Man with his paired character.
 
 The character arriving in front receives a shield.
 
 If Old Man enters front, he shields himself.
 
 If Old Man retreats, the incoming ally receives the shield.

## Chaos Sorcerer awakening

 Sleep meter is removed.
 
 Old Man and alter remain as two combat states.
 
 A skill switches between them.
 
 Switching does not end the turn.
 
 Alter becomes fully controllable.
 
 Both Old Man and alter have different front/reserve effects.
 
 Narrative acceptance of the alter turns involuntary loss of control into voluntary cooperation.

## Arcana Grandmaster awakening

 Sleep meter is removed.
 
 Alter no longer exists.
 
 Spells become significantly more powerful.
 
 Spells continue improving through progression.
 
 Powerful spells cost health.
 
 Spell cannot be cast without sufficient health.
 
 Casting cannot directly kill the Old Man.
 
 His circulatory system visibly glows as arcana in his blood becomes dangerously amplified.
 
 Grandmaster skills also have distinct front/reserve effects.
